Argument 1 (For)Technology keeps teenagers away from nature

Main idea 1

Technology reduces time outdoors because teenagers prefer social media and games.

Expert opinion. Richard Louv, in Last Child in the Woods (2005), explains that electronic media pull children indoors and calls the result "nature-deficit disorder," which is linked to stress and poor health.

Main idea 2

Technology changes how teenagers experience nature, as they focus on taking photos instead of enjoying the moment.

Expert opinion. Psychologist Linda Henkel of Fairfield University (Psychological Science, 2014) found a "photo-taking-impairment effect": people remember fewer objects and details when they photograph them instead of simply observing them.

Argument 2 (Against)Technology helps teenagers connect with nature

Main idea 1

Technology helps teenagers learn about nature through apps like iNaturalist.

Expert opinion. Researchers from the Public University of Navarre, Spain (Sustainability, 2021) trained 122 sixteen-year-old students to record plants with iNaturalist during a field trip. The students recorded 32 species, and the researchers concluded that iNaturalist is a valuable tool for learning about biodiversity.

Main idea 2

Technology inspires outdoor activities, as social media content and apps like AllTrails encourage hiking and camping.

Expert opinion. Tim Althoff (Stanford University), Ryen White, and Eric Horvitz (Microsoft Research) studied 32,000 users in 2016 and found that the outdoor game Pokémon Go increased very active players' walking by about 1,473 steps a day, more than 25% above their usual level.
